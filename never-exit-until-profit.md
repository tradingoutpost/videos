# This Trading Rule Makes Losing Almost Impossible

{% raw %}
Every figure, price, level and count the finished picture puts on screen, and where it came
from. Published on the channel's site, so each citation here is a public one.

## What this video claims

The video makes a single argument about how traders treat the exit button. It is an argument
about behaviour and about how a rule is worded, and it is put entirely in the abstract: it
names no product, no platform, no study, no win rate, no backtest and no dated event, and it
does not say that anything happened.

That matters for this file, because it means there is almost nothing here of the kind this
file usually holds. There are three numbers in the whole script and none of them is offered
as evidence:

| Said | Where | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "down two percent" | Chapter one, twice in one sentence | A hypothetical, used to hold one variable still while the other changes. The same trade is described closed and not closed. No instrument, date or account is named. |
| "forty seven exceptions" | Chapter one | Rhetorical exaggeration for a rule book that has grown too many clauses. Not a count of anything. |
| "ninety seconds" | Chapter five | Part of the cooking comparison, not a claim about markets. |

## Figures on screen

Every price series, level, profit figure, pip count, drawdown and equity curve in the
picture is **illustrative**: invented from a seeded model to show a mechanism, not a record
of anything that happened. Each chart pane says `illustrative` beneath it for that reason.

Every figure drawn against a series is computed from that series at the frame it is drawn
on, rather than typed next to it, so a retune of a series moves its numbers with it. The
instrument names on the panes (EURUSD, GBPUSD, GBPJPY, AUDUSD, XAUUSD, US30) are titles on
invented data and are labelled as such; nothing in the video asserts that any of these
instruments did what the panes show.

No performance figure, win rate, hit rate, expectancy or account outcome is stated anywhere
in the narration or drawn as a result. The one place a "closed trade history" appears, both
versions of it are invented and shown side by side to compare two rules for closing, not to
report a record.

## Marks used on screen

Two real brand marks appear, in the passage listing the markets the rule can be carried
into. Both are the published geometry rather than a redrawing.

- Nvidia and Bitcoin marks: the official paths distributed by the simple-icons project,
  https://simpleicons.org
- Forex, futures and options have no mark anywhere and are drawn as glyphs built from what
  the instrument is, rather than being given an invented logo.

## Not checked

- The video's central proposition, that refusing to close a position until it shows a good
  amount of profit is a sound way to trade, is the script's own argument and is not
  something this file can source. It is stated in the script as an argument and is not
  supported here by evidence.
- No claim about how many traders behave in any of the ways described has been chased to a
  source, because the script does not make one: it says "most traders" and "a lot of
  traders" without a figure, and nothing on screen supplies one.
