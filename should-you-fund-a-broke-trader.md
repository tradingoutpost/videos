# The Strangest Trading Request You Should Answer

{% raw %}
Every figure, name and mechanism the finished picture puts on screen, chased to a primary
source. The video argues a case rather than reporting one, so most of what it says is
reasoning about a hypothetical rather than a claim about the world. That makes the short
list below the whole of what is checkable, and it makes the second half of this file, the
figures that are illustrative rather than asserted, the more important half.

## What the video names

### Prop firm challenges

The script says a small amount might be "enough to start a small prop challenge, fund a
tiny account, pay for data, or take the first position". A prop firm challenge is a paid
evaluation: a trader pays a fee, trades a simulated account to a profit target inside
drawdown limits, and on passing is given a funded account and a share of the profits.

- FTMO, "How it works": https://ftmo.com/en/how-it-works/
- The Funded Trader, evaluation rules: https://thefundedtraderprogram.com/

The video puts no fee, no pass rate and no payout split on screen, and states none. The
size of a challenge fee varies by firm and by account size, and the video's shot draws the
challenge as an object rather than pricing it.

### Funded account certificates

Chapter four's "funded account certificate" is the pass confirmation a prop firm issues.
The shot draws a certificate and a seal and prints no issuer, no figure and no firm name.

- FTMO, "FTMO Account": https://ftmo.com/en/ftmo-account/

### The instruments named in the narration

A candle, a moving average and an order book each appear as a drawn glyph rather than a
logo, because none of the three is a product and none of them has a mark. Their drawings
are of the mechanism itself: an order book as resting bids and offers either side of a
midpoint, a moving average as a smoothed line lagging a price line, a candle as a body and
two wicks.

- CME Group, order book and market depth:
  https://www.cmegroup.com/education/courses/market-regulation/market-depth.html
- CME Group, reading candlestick charts:
  https://www.cmegroup.com/education/courses/technical-analysis/understanding-candlestick-charts.html

### The platform marks that appear

TradingView, Binance and Robinhood appear as real marks in two shots: a browser tab strip
in "someone posts", and the data feed in "what that money buys". They are drawn from the
simple-icons geometry the channel already carries, never approximated, and they identify a
category the narration names rather than making any claim about the companies. No claim is
made about any of the three, and none of them is connected to the argument.

## Figures on screen that are illustrative, and not asserted

The video's whole subject is a hypothetical: somebody asks strangers for a small amount and
somebody answers. The picture has to show that amount and that outcome, so several numbers
appear that describe the thought experiment rather than the world. They are listed here so
that nothing on screen is left unaccounted for.

- **$50 and $100.** The script's own amounts, spoken in chapter one and chapter six. Every
  other money figure on screen is one of these two.
- **Every price chart in the video is synthetic.** The candles, equity curves and decay
  curves are generated from a deterministic walk in `src/lib/chart.tsx` so the same series
  draws identically on every render. None of them is a real instrument, none is labelled as
  one, and no price axis carries a currency or a symbol.
- **The market tape carries symbols and no prices.** It reads EURUSD, BTCUSD, US500 and so
  on, with no figures beside them. A tape carrying prices reads as live market data, and a
  live price is a figure this video cannot source and does not need.
- **The dial readings.** The gauges in "not a genius but", "could be less skilled",
  "it can make them careful", "humility and specificity", "before the result it costs" and
  "one person in the background" are diagrammatic: they read a person in the video's own
  thought experiment, on scales the video invents in the same breath. They are not
  measurements of anything and no source exists or could exist for them.
- **The three instruments in "what traders actually measure" print no figures.** The rings
  fill and the labels name return, drawdown and win rate. An earlier version printed
  percentages, which would have read as a statistic about traders in general.
- **The marketplace price tags carry a currency mark and no amount.** The beat's claim is
  that everything in these spaces is for sale, which a tag says on its own; an amount would
  have been invented pricing for real product categories.
- **The account figures inside depicted screenshots.** "Balance becomes personality" and
  "they love the screenshot" show an account balance and a small to large result inside a
  drawn screenshot frame. The frame is the point: they depict what somebody posts, and are
  not offered as anybody's real result.
- **The reply and score counts** on the forum shots are part of a drawn thread and describe
  no real post.

## Not chased to a source, because there is nothing to chase

The argument itself is not a factual claim and is not treated as one anywhere in the
picture. That gratitude tends to follow help, that being trusted can change how carefully
somebody behaves, and that a relationship formed before a result differs from one formed
after it, are the video's reasoning. The shots draw them as diagrams of the argument, and
none of them is presented as a measured effect or given a figure.
